This is my portfolio for PP434 Automated Data Visualisation for Policymaking. It demonstrates the tools and techniques covered in class, building toward my main project.

01

CC1: Hosting a Live Website

Chart 1: ETH Price vs. Volume

A live, auto-updating chart tracking Ether's price against trading volume. This demonstrates pulling in a continuously refreshed data feed and plotting two series together using separate axes.

Chart 2: Inflation Across the G7

A multi-line chart comparing inflation rates across four G7 countries over time, sourced from OECD data. This demonstrates plotting several comparable series on one chart to make cross-country trends easy to read at a glance.

Chart 3: Bitcoin High/Low Trading Range

A filled-range chart showing the gap between Bitcoin's daily high and low trading price. This demonstrates using a filled area between two lines to highlight volatility or spread, a technique I plan to adapt for showing variation across prison facilities in my main project.

02

CC2: Building from an API

Chart 4: Renewable Energy Output in the US

Annual renewable energy output (GWh) in the United States, sourced via the Economics Observatory API. Since the live API doesn't allow direct browser access, this chart runs from a cached snapshot of the API response stored in this repository — a common workaround for CORS restrictions on public data APIs.

Chart 5: Combined Crime Offences in Orange County, FL (2004–2020)

Combined violent and property crime offences known to law enforcement in Orange County, Florida. This chart draws on my undergraduate background in criminology and will inform the criminal justice framing of my main project.

03

CC3: Policy Debate

Has reducing US imprisonment rates been accompanied by rising crime, undermining the case for continued decarceration?

Imprisonment rates fell from 506 to 360 per 100,000 between 2008 and 2023 — a sustained, two-decade decline in incarceration.

Violent crime fell over the same period, from 463.6 to 385.6 per 100,000 — moving with imprisonment rates, not against them.
