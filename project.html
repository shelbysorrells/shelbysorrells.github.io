The Great Scrubbing

How DEI language disappeared from corporate America's SEC filings

Photo via Magnific (Premium license)

Aims

In 2025, companies across the US quietly began removing references to diversity, equity, and inclusion from their public communications — but how much of that retreat actually reached the one place companies can't easily spin: their legally mandated disclosures to investors? This project asks a specific, countable question: did the exact language companies use in SEC filings measurably change after the 2024 US election and the Trump administration's subsequent anti-DEI executive orders, and if so, how fast and how far?

Using the SEC's EDGAR full-text search system — which indexes the complete text of every public filing since 2001 — I track how often specific terms ("diversity, equity, and inclusion," "ESG," "climate risk") appear in annual 10-K filings, quarter by quarter, before and after this political shift. An initial test of one phrase alone showed a striking year-over-year drop of over 50% between Q1 2024 and Q1 2025 — large enough to warrant closer examination across multiple terms and a longer time window.

This matters because SEC filings are the one corporate communication channel where companies face real legal exposure for misstatement, making any detectable shift in language a meaningful signal of how deeply political pressure has reached into formal corporate governance, not just public messaging.

Notes for replication

Data gathered via the SEC EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index), searching the exact phrase "diversity, equity, and inclusion" within 10-K filings, filtered to the first quarter of each year for a fair comparison (10-K filing volume is heavily concentrated in Q1 due to fiscal year-end deadlines, so comparing Q1-to-Q1 avoids a seasonality bias that comparing different quarters would introduce). Because this API blocks direct browser requests (CORS), data was collected via a Python script run in Google Colab and saved as a static file.
