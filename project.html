The Great Scrubbing

How DEI language disappeared from corporate America's SEC filings

Photo via Magnific (Premium license)

Aims

In 2025, companies across the US quietly began removing references to diversity, equity, and inclusion from their public communications — but how much of that retreat actually reached the one place companies can't easily spin: their legally mandated disclosures to investors? This project asks a specific, countable question: did the exact language companies use in SEC filings measurably change after the 2024 US election and the Trump administration's subsequent anti-DEI executive orders, and if so, how fast and how far?

Using the SEC's EDGAR full-text search system — which indexes the complete text of every public filing since 2001 — I track how often specific terms ("diversity, equity, and inclusion," "ESG," "climate risk") appear in annual 10-K filings, before and after this political shift.

This matters because SEC filings are the one corporate communication channel where companies face real legal exposure for misstatement, making any detectable shift in language a meaningful signal of how deeply political pressure has reached into formal corporate governance, not just public messaging.

Mentions of "diversity, equity, and inclusion" in 10-K filings dropped from 1,187 in Q1 2024, to 571 in Q1 2025, to just 337 in Q1 2026 — a continuous decline across three consecutive years.

Notes for replication

Data gathered via the SEC EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index), searching the exact phrase "diversity, equity, and inclusion" within 10-K filings, filtered to the first quarter of each year for a fair comparison (10-K filing volume is heavily concentrated in Q1 due to fiscal year-end deadlines). Because this API blocks direct browser requests (CORS), data was collected via a Python script run in Google Colab and saved as a static file. Figures reflect the index as queried on 10 November 2026; SEC's search index is periodically reprocessed, so the same historical date range can return slightly different counts if queried again later.

The "% Change" view shows which states saw the sharpest collapse in DEI language, relative to their own starting point. The "Q1 2024" and "Q1 2026" views show the underlying raw counts behind that calculation.

Notes for replication

State-level filing counts gathered via the SEC EDGAR full-text search API, querying each US state individually using the locationCodes parameter (filtering by principal business address) to get an exact count per state, rather than relying on the API's own aggregation feature, which caps out at roughly 30 states. Data was collected via a Python script run in Google Colab due to the same CORS restriction noted above. ESG and climate-risk language declined too, but far more gently than DEI, suggesting companies weren't just growing cautious about political language in general. DEI specifically was the target.

Notes for replication

Each term is shown as a percentage of its own historical peak (2023 for DEI and ESG; 2024 for Climate Risk), since the three terms appear in very different numbers of filings to begin with — normalizing lets the shape of each term's rise and reversal be compared directly. Data gathered via the SEC EDGAR full-text search API, same method as the first chart.

Fifteen named companies, each tracked individually in their own SEC filings, sorted by when they dropped the language. Tesla moved first, in 2023, even before the 2024 election. Most of corporate America followed between 2024 and 2026. Target is the sole holdout, using the phrase in every filing through 2026.

Notes for replication

23 companies were tested in total, drawn from a Harvard Law School corporate governance report on DEI disclosure and multiple news outlets (TechCrunch, Fortune, Axios) reporting on specific companies' DEI rollbacks. Eight (Microsoft, Intel, ExxonMobil, Lowe's, Boeing, UnitedHealth Group, Kroger, General Electric) showed zero mentions in every single year under either search term and were excluded from this chart, since this almost certainly reflects different company-specific wording rather than a genuine absence of DEI language — a limitation worth noting for any full-text search approach like this one.

Each company's 10-K filings queried individually by CIK (SEC's company identifier) via the EDGAR full-text search API, checking each full calendar year for either the phrase "diversity, equity, and inclusion" or "DEI."
